# **Unit IV – Cryptocurrency and Token**

## **Topic 2: Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency**

### **1. What is Bitcoin?**

**Bitcoin** is a decentralized digital cryptocurrency that allows people to send and receive money directly over a peer-to-peer network without requiring a central authority, such as a bank.

- Introduced in 2009 by the pseudonymous creator Satoshi Nakamoto.
- It is based on blockchain technology.
- Transactions are recorded on a public, distributed ledger.
- It uses cryptographic techniques to secure transactions.
- It uses the Proof of Work (PoW) consensus mechanism.
- Its maximum supply is limited to **21 million bitcoins**.

### **2. Bitcoin and cryptocurrency relationship**

- **Cryptocurrency** is the broader category of digital currencies secured using cryptography.
- **Bitcoin** is one example of a cryptocurrency.
- Bitcoin was the first widely adopted decentralized cryptocurrency.
- Other cryptocurrencies include Ethereum, Litecoin and Ripple (XRP).

1. **Transaction creation:** The sender enters the receiver's Bitcoin address and amount.
2. **Digital signature:** The sender signs the transaction using their private key.
3. **Broadcasting:** The transaction is broadcast to the Bitcoin network.
4. **Verification:** Network nodes verify the transaction.
5. **Mining:** Miners compete to solve a computational puzzle using Proof of Work.
6. **Block addition:** A valid block is added to the blockchain.
7. **Confirmation:** The transaction receives confirmations as subsequent blocks are added.

### **5. Advantages of Bitcoin**

1. Decentralized – no central authority controls the network.
2. Transparency – transactions are recorded on a public blockchain.
3. Security – cryptography protects transactions.
4. Global transfers – can be sent across borders.
5. Limited supply – maximum supply of 21 million BTC.
6. User control – users can control their funds through private keys.

### **6. Limitations of Bitcoin**

1. Price volatility.
2. Transaction confirmation can take time.
3. Mining consumes significant electricity.
4. Lost private keys can result in loss of access to funds.
5. Regulatory and legal treatment varies between countries.
