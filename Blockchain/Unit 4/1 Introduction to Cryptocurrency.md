Topic 1: Introduction to Cryptocurrency
1. What is Cryptocurrency?
Cryptocurrency is a form of digital or virtual currency that uses cryptography to secure transactions and control the creation or transfer of digital units.

Most cryptocurrencies operate on blockchain or other distributed ledger technologies, allowing transactions to be recorded and verified across a network.

Examples include Bitcoin and Ethereum.
2. Important characteristics
1. Digital: Exists electronically; it is not physical cash.
2. Cryptographic security: Uses cryptographic techniques to secure transactions.
3. Decentralisation: Many cryptocurrencies operate without a central bank or single controlling authority.
4. Transparency: Public blockchain transactions can be viewed and verified.
5. Peer-to-peer transactions: Users can transfer cryptocurrency directly through the network.
6. Immutability: Confirmed blockchain records are difficult to alter.
7. Global accessibility: Transactions can be made across geographical boundaries, subject to applicable laws and network access.
3. How does cryptocurrency work?

1. A user initiates a cryptocurrency transaction.
2. The transaction is digitally signed using the sender's private key.
3. The transaction is broadcast to the network.
4. Network nodes verify the transaction.
5. The transaction is included in a block according to the network's consensus mechanism.
6. The block is added to the blockchain.
7. The receiver's wallet reflects the transaction after the required confirmations.
4. Advantages
- Fast digital transfers, depending on the network.
- Peer-to-peer transfer capability.
- Global accessibility.
- Cryptographic protection.
- Transparent transaction records on public blockchains.
- Potentially lower dependence on traditional intermediaries.
5. Disadvantages
- Price volatility.
- Risk of scams and phishing.
- Loss of funds if private keys are lost or stolen.
- Regulatory uncertainty and restrictions in different countries.
- Network fees and congestion.
- Transactions may be difficult or impossible to reverse.
