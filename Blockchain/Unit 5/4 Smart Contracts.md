## **Topic 4: Introduction to Smart Contracts**

### **1. What is a Smart Contract?**

A **smart contract** is a self-executing digital contract in which the terms and conditions are written in computer code and deployed on a blockchain.

It automatically executes predefined instructions when specified conditions are satisfied, without requiring a central authority or intermediary.

**Example:** Suppose a customer purchases a product online. Once the payment is received and verified, the smart contract can automatically release the payment to the seller.

### **2. Features of Smart Contracts**

1. **Self-executing:** Executes automatically when predefined conditions are met.
2. **Decentralized:** Runs on a blockchain network rather than a central server.
3. **Transparency:** Contract rules and transactions can be verified on the blockchain.
4. **Security:** Uses cryptographic techniques and blockchain mechanisms.
5. **Immutable:** Once deployed, the contract's code is generally difficult to modify.
6. **Accuracy:** Executes predefined instructions without manual intervention.
7. **Cost reduction:** Reduces the need for intermediaries.
8. **Speed:** Automates processes that might otherwise require manual verification.

### **3. How does a Smart Contract work?**

## **Working of a Smart Contract**

**1. Contract creation**

Developer writes the contract code.

**2. Contract deployment**

Contract is deployed on the blockchain.

**3. Condition verification**

Specified conditions are checked when triggered.

**4. Automatic execution**

EVM executes the contract instructions.

**5. Result recorded**

The resulting state change is recorded on the blockchain.

### **4. Example of a Smart Contract**

**Online payment system**

Consider an online shopping platform.

- Buyer selects a product.
- Buyer sends the payment.
- Smart contract verifies the payment.
- When the required condition is satisfied, the contract releases the payment to the seller.
- The transaction is recorded on the blockchain.

This reduces manual processing and the need for an intermediary.

### **5. Role of EVM in Smart Contracts**

The Ethereum Virtual Machine plays an important role in smart contract execution.

- Executes smart contract bytecode.
- Processes contract instructions.
- Checks and applies state changes.
- Uses gas to measure computational resources.
- Ensures execution follows Ethereum's rules.

### **6. Advantages of Smart Contracts**

| Advantage       | Explanation                                     |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Automation      | Executes automatically when conditions are met. |
| Transparency    | Transactions can be verified.                   |
| Security        | Uses cryptographic and blockchain mechanisms.   |
| Cost efficiency | Reduces intermediary costs.                     |
| Speed           | Reduces manual processing time.                 |
| Accuracy        | Follows predefined program instructions.        |

### **7. Limitations of Smart Contracts**

- Bugs in code may cause unexpected results.
- Deployed code can be difficult to change.
- Deployment and execution require gas fees on Ethereum.
- Legal recognition and enforceability can vary.
- External real-world information may require oracles.
