## **1. Introduction to IOTA**

IOTA is a distributed ledger technology designed for the Internet of Things ecosystem.

It enables connected devices to communicate, exchange data and transfer value without depending on a traditional centralized intermediary.

IOTA was introduced in 2015 by a group of founders including David Sønstebø, Sergey Ivancheglo, Dominik Schiener and Serguei Popov.

Its original architecture used the Tangle instead of a conventional blockchain.

2. **IoT-focused:** Designed for communication between connected devices.
3. **Scalability:** Its DAG-based design aims to support parallel transaction processing.
4. **Decentralization:** Designed to support distributed participation.
5. **Data integrity:** Uses cryptographic mechanisms to protect data.
6. **Machine-to-machine communication:** Enables connected devices to exchange information.
7. **Microtransactions:** Designed to support small-value transactions between devices.
8. **Distributed ledger:** Maintains transaction information across participating nodes.

## **3. IOTA Architecture – Tangle**

Most Important Concept

### **What is Tangle?**

Tangle is a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)-based distributed ledger structure used in the original IOTA architecture.

In a traditional blockchain, transactions are grouped into blocks that are connected sequentially.

In Tangle, transactions are represented as vertices in a graph, and their relationships are represented by directed edges.

### **Characteristics of Tangle**

- Transactions are represented as individual vertices.
- Transactions are connected through directed edges.
- It does not use the traditional sequential block structure.
- Transactions can be processed in parallel.
- New transactions approve previous transactions.
- Its design aims to support IoT microtransactions.

### **Steps involved**

1. **Transaction initiation:** An IoT device creates a transaction.
2. **Transaction selection:** The system selects previous transactions that need approval.
3. **Verification:** The selected transactions are checked for validity.
4. **Approval:** The new transaction approves the selected previous transactions.
5. **Network propagation:** The transaction is propagated through the network.
6. **Confirmation:** The network's protocol determines when the transaction is considered confirmed.

**Important:** In the original IOTA Tangle design, a new transaction approved two previous transactions. Do not assume that this simplified description applies unchanged to every version of IOTA.
