# **Unit VI – Blockchain Case Studies**

## **Topic 7: Blockchain in Energy and Utilities**

### **1. Introduction**

The energy and utilities sector includes electricity generation, distribution, consumption, and management of resources such as electricity, gas, and water.

Blockchain can be used in this sector to maintain transparent energy transaction records, support peer-to-peer energy trading, and automate certain operations through smart contracts.

### **2. Applications of Blockchain in Energy and Utilities**

**1. Peer-to-Peer (P2P) Energy Trading**

- Consumers who generate extra electricity using solar panels can sell it to other consumers.
- Blockchain records energy transactions between buyers and sellers.
- Smart contracts can automate payment according to agreed conditions.

**2. Smart Grid Management**

- Blockchain can support secure sharing of information among energy producers, distributors, and consumers.
- Helps maintain transparent records of energy transactions.
- Can support decentralized energy management.

**3. Renewable Energy Tracking**

- Blockchain can record information about electricity generated from renewable sources.
- Helps track renewable energy certificates.
- Provides traceability of renewable energy transactions.

**4. Smart Metering**

- Smart meters collect electricity consumption data.
- Blockchain can maintain tamper-resistant records of meter readings.
- Helps improve transparency in billing and energy usage.

**5. Automated Billing and Payments**

- Smart contracts can calculate payments based on recorded energy consumption.
- Can automate certain billing and settlement processes.
- Reduces manual work.

**6. Electric Vehicle (EV) Charging**

- Blockchain can record charging transactions between EV owners and charging stations.
- Supports transparent billing and payment records.
- Can help coordinate charging services across different providers.

### **3. Working of Peer-to-Peer Energy Trading**

## **Blockchain-based energy trading**

**Solar Energy Producer**

Generates electricity and has excess energy

**Blockchain Network**

Records and validates the energy transaction

**Smart Contract**

Checks agreed conditions and processes payment

**Energy Consumer**

Purchases electricity from the producer

**Explanation:**

1. A household generates electricity using solar panels.
2. Excess electricity is made available for trading.
3. A consumer agrees to purchase the electricity.
4. Blockchain records the transaction.
5. A smart contract processes payment according to the agreed conditions.

*Note: Blockchain records and coordinates transactions; the actual electricity flow is managed by the physical electricity grid.*

### **4. Benefits of Blockchain in Energy and Utilities**

| Benefit                   | Explanation                                           |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Transparency              | Provides traceable energy transaction records.        |
| Decentralization          | Supports energy trading between participants.         |
| Automation                | Smart contracts automate billing and payments.        |
| Security                  | Cryptographic techniques protect transaction records. |
| Efficiency                | Can reduce manual processes and intermediaries.       |
| Renewable energy tracking | Helps verify renewable energy transactions.           |

### **5. Challenges**

1. **Scalability:** Energy systems may generate a large number of transactions.
2. **Regulatory issues:** Energy trading must follow government regulations.
3. **Infrastructure cost:** Blockchain implementation requires investment.
4. **Energy consumption:** Some consensus mechanisms consume significant computing energy.
5. **Integration:** Blockchain must work with existing electricity grids and smart meters.

### **6. Example**

Suppose a household has rooftop solar panels and generates more electricity than it needs.

- The household offers excess electricity for sale.
- A nearby consumer agrees to purchase it.
- A smart meter measures the energy transferred.
- Blockchain records the transaction.
- A smart contract processes the payment.

This is an example of peer-to-peer energy trading.
