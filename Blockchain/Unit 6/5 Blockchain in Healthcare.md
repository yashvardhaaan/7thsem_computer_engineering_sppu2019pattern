# **Unit VI – Blockchain Case Studies**

## **Topic 5: Blockchain in Healthcare**

### **1. Introduction**

Healthcare organizations manage sensitive information such as patient medical records, prescriptions, laboratory reports, insurance details, and treatment history.

Blockchain can help healthcare organizations maintain secure, traceable, and tamper-resistant records. It allows authorized participants, such as hospitals, laboratories, doctors, and pharmacies, to share verified information.

### **2. Applications of Blockchain in Healthcare**

**1. Electronic Health Records (EHR)**

- Patient medical records can be linked to blockchain-based records.
- Authorized hospitals and doctors can access relevant patient information.
- Helps maintain a consistent medical history across healthcare providers.

**2. Secure Sharing of Medical Data**

- Hospitals, laboratories, and doctors can share medical information securely.
- Access permissions can control who is allowed to view patient records.
- Reduces duplication of medical records.

**3. Drug Supply Chain Management**

- Blockchain can track medicines from manufacturers to distributors, pharmacies, and hospitals.
- Records product origin, batch number, and movement.
- Helps detect counterfeit or unauthorized medicines.

**4. Clinical Trials**

- Clinical trial data and study-related records can be timestamped.
- Helps maintain transparency and traceability of trial information.
- Makes unauthorized changes to recorded information easier to detect.

**5. Health Insurance Management**

- Blockchain can maintain records of insurance claims and transactions.
- Smart contracts can automate certain claim-processing steps.
- Can reduce paperwork and duplicate claims.

**6. Patient Identity Management**

- Blockchain can help verify patient identities.
- Supports secure access to medical records.
- Helps reduce identity-related errors and fraud.

**7. Medical Research**

- Researchers can share verified datasets and research records.
- Supports traceability of data used in research.
- Access permissions can help protect sensitive information.

### **3. Working of Blockchain in Healthcare**

## **Medical record sharing using blockchain**

**Patient**

Provides medical information and grants access permissions

**Hospital / Laboratory**

Creates or updates medical records

**Blockchain Network**

Records transactions and verifies data integrity

**Authorized Healthcare Provider**

Accesses permitted patient information

*Note: In practical healthcare systems, large medical files are often stored off-chain. Blockchain may store hashes, permissions, and references rather than the complete medical records.*

### **4. Benefits of Blockchain in Healthcare**

| Benefit          | Explanation                                                        |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Data security    | Cryptographic techniques help protect medical records.             |
| Data integrity   | Makes unauthorized modification easier to detect.                  |
| Interoperability | Supports information sharing between healthcare organizations.     |
| Transparency     | Provides traceable records of data-related activities.             |
| Patient control  | Access permissions can give patients greater control over sharing. |
| Reduced fraud    | Helps verify medicine supply chains and insurance claims.          |
| Efficiency       | Can reduce duplication and manual administrative work.             |

### **5. Challenges of Blockchain in Healthcare**

1. **Privacy:** Medical records contain highly sensitive personal information.
2. **Scalability:** Healthcare systems generate large amounts of data.
3. **Cost:** Implementing blockchain infrastructure can be expensive.
4. **Integration:** Existing hospital systems may be difficult to connect.
5. **Regulatory compliance:** Healthcare data must follow applicable privacy and data-protection laws.
6. **Data accuracy:** Blockchain cannot guarantee that incorrect information entered initially is correct.

### **6. Example**

Suppose a patient visits Hospital A and later visits Hospital B.

1. Hospital A creates the patient's medical record.
2. A reference or cryptographic hash of the record is associated with the blockchain.
3. The patient authorizes Hospital B to access the relevant information.
4. Hospital B verifies the record and accesses the permitted medical data.
5. The patient's treatment history can be used without repeating every test unnecessarily.
