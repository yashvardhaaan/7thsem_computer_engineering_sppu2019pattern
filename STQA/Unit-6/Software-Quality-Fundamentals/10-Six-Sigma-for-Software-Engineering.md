## 2. What is Six Sigma?

Six Sigma is a data-driven quality management methodology used to improve process quality by identifying and eliminating defects, reducing variations and continuously improving processes.

It aims to achieve a very low defect rate of approximately 3.4 defects per million opportunities (DPMO) under the conventional Six Sigma performance assumption.

In Software Quality Assurance, Six Sigma is used to improve software development and testing processes by reducing defects and improving product reliability.

Example: A software company uses Six Sigma techniques to identify the reasons for repeated software failures and reduce the number of defects released to customers.

## 3. Characteristics of Six Sigma

1\. Customer Focus

- Focuses on understanding and satisfying customer requirements.
- Improves software quality according to customer expectations.

2\. Data-Driven Approach

- Decisions are based on facts, measurements and statistical analysis.
- Uses defect reports, test results and quality metrics.

3\. Defect Reduction

- Identifies and eliminates the causes of defects.
- Aims to reduce errors in software development.

4\. Process Improvement

- Continuously improves software development and testing processes.
- Identifies inefficient activities and removes unnecessary steps.

5\. Reduction in Process Variation

- Reduces variations in software development activities.
- Helps maintain consistent software quality.

6\. Continuous Improvement

- Encourages regular monitoring and improvement of processes.
- Ensures long-term quality enhancement.

7\. Employee Involvement

- Involves employees and management in quality improvement activities.
- Promotes teamwork and responsibility.

8\. Measurement and Statistical Analysis

- Uses statistical tools to measure process performance.
- Helps identify the root causes of quality problems.

DMAIC stands for:

### 1. Define

- Identify the software quality problem.
- Define customer requirements and project objectives.
- Determine the scope of improvement.

Example: Identify that the software application has a high login failure rate.

### 2. Measure

- Collect data about the existing process.
- Measure defect rates, failure rates and testing performance.
- Establish the current quality level.

Example: Record the number of login failures per 1,000 login attempts.

### 3. Analyze

- Analyse collected data to identify the root causes of defects.
- Use techniques such as cause-and-effect diagrams and Pareto analysis.
- Identify the main reasons for software failures.

Example: Discover that most login failures occur due to incorrect session handling.

### 4. Improve

- Develop and implement solutions to remove identified causes.
- Modify the development or testing process.
- Verify whether the improvements reduce defects.

Example: Improve session management and introduce additional automated test cases.

### 5. Control

- Monitor the improved process continuously.
- Maintain quality standards.
- Use quality metrics and regular audits to prevent defects from recurring.

Example: Continuously monitor login failure rates after deploying the improvement.

## 6. Advantages of Six Sigma in SQA

1. Reduces software defects and failures.
2. Improves software reliability and quality.
3. Increases customer satisfaction.
4. Reduces development and maintenance costs.
5. Improves efficiency of software testing processes.
6. Supports continuous process improvement.
7. Encourages decisions based on measurable data.

## 7. Conclusion

Six Sigma is a data-driven quality improvement methodology that focuses on reducing defects, minimising process variations and improving customer satisfaction. In Software Quality Assurance, it uses the DMAIC approach — Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve and Control — to improve software development and testing processes.
