## 2. What is Total Quality Management (TQM)?

Total Quality Management (TQM) is an organisation-wide management approach that focuses on continuously improving the quality of products, services and processes to achieve customer satisfaction.

It involves participation from all employees, from top-level management to workers, to improve organisational performance and prevent defects.

In Software Quality Assurance, TQM focuses on improving the entire software development process to deliver reliable, high-quality software that satisfies customer requirements.

Example: A software company involves developers, testers, project managers and customers in continuously improving its software development and testing processes.

## 3. Principles of TQM

1\. Customer Focus

- Customer satisfaction is the primary objective.
- Software products should meet customer requirements and expectations.

2\. Total Employee Involvement

- Every employee is responsible for maintaining quality.
- Employees participate in quality improvement activities.

3\. Continuous Improvement

- Processes and products should be improved continuously.
- Regular reviews and feedback help improve software quality.

4\. Process-Centred Approach

- Quality is achieved by improving the processes used to develop products.
- Standardised processes help reduce errors.

5\. Integrated System

- All departments and activities work together towards common quality objectives.
- Development, testing and maintenance teams coordinate their activities.

6\. Fact-Based Decision Making

- Decisions are based on data, measurements and analysis.
- Quality metrics help identify problems and improvements.

7\. Leadership Commitment

- Management establishes quality objectives and provides resources.
- Leaders promote a quality-oriented organisational culture.

8\. Effective Communication

- Clear communication between employees and departments.
- Helps identify problems and resolve them quickly.

## 4. Components of TQM

TQM can be explained using the following important components:

1. Quality Planning: Establish quality objectives, standards and procedures.
2. Quality Assurance: Ensure that appropriate processes are followed to achieve quality.
3. Quality Control: Identify defects and verify whether the product meets requirements.
4. Quality Improvement: Continuously improve products and processes.

## 6. Implementation of TQM in Software Quality Assurance

Step 1: Establish Quality Objectives

\- Define software quality standards and customer expectations.

Step 2: Management Commitment

\- Management provides resources, training and support for quality activities.

Step 3: Employee Training

\- Train developers, testers and other employees in quality practices.

Step 4: Process Improvement

\- Identify inefficient processes and improve software development activities.

Step 5: Quality Measurement

\- Use metrics such as defect density, test coverage and customer satisfaction.

Step 6: Continuous Monitoring

\- Conduct regular reviews, audits and feedback sessions to maintain quality.

## 7. Advantages of TQM

1. Improves software quality and reliability.
2. Increases customer satisfaction.
3. Reduces defects and rework.
4. Reduces development and maintenance costs.
5. Improves employee participation and teamwork.
6. Increases organisational efficiency.
7. Encourages continuous improvement.

## 8. Conclusion

Total Quality Management is an organisation-wide approach that focuses on continuous quality improvement and customer satisfaction. It involves all employees, management commitment, process improvement and fact-based decision-making. In Software Quality Assurance, TQM helps organisations develop reliable software and maintain consistent quality.

## Difference Between TQM and Six Sigma

This comparison is useful because both topics are repeatedly asked in your SQA papers.

| TQM                                           | Six Sigma                                      |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Organisation-wide quality management approach | Data-driven quality improvement methodology    |
| Focuses on overall quality                    | Focuses on reducing defects and variation      |
| Involves all employees                        | Uses trained improvement teams and specialists |
| Emphasises continuous improvement             | Uses structured DMAIC methodology              |
| Customer satisfaction is central              | Defect reduction and customer satisfaction     |
