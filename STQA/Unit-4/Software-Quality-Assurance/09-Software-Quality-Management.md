## Q1. Explain Quality Management System (QMS). (Nov 2022, May 2025 – 6 Marks)

Definition:

A Quality Management System (QMS) is a structured system of policies, processes, procedures and responsibilities implemented by an organisation to ensure that its products and services consistently meet customer requirements and quality standards.

In software engineering, QMS helps ensure that software is developed, tested and delivered according to defined quality requirements.

Objectives of QMS:

1. To maintain consistent software quality.
2. To satisfy customer requirements.
3. To prevent defects and reduce errors.
4. To improve development processes.
5. To ensure compliance with quality standards.
6. To promote continuous improvement.

Main Components of QMS:

1\. Quality Policy: Defines the organisation's commitment towards quality.

2\. Quality Planning: Identifies quality objectives, standards and procedures.

3\. Quality Assurance: Ensures that proper processes are followed to prevent defects.

4\. Quality Control: Checks the actual product to identify defects.

5\. Quality Improvement: Continuously improves processes and product quality.

6\. Documentation: Maintains quality records, procedures and reports.

Example:

Consider a software company developing an online banking application.

The company implements QMS by:

- Defining coding standards.
- Establishing software testing procedures.
- Conducting code reviews.
- Performing security testing.
- Collecting customer feedback.
- Improving development processes based on identified issues.

Advantages of QMS:

1. Improves software quality.
2. Increases customer satisfaction.
3. Reduces development costs.
4. Improves organisational efficiency.
5. Ensures consistent processes.
6. Supports continuous improvement.

Conclusion: QMS provides a systematic approach to managing and improving software quality while ensuring customer satisfaction and compliance with defined standards.

## Q2. Explain the Pillars of Quality Management System. (Nov 2023 – 5 Marks)

Definition:

The pillars of Quality Management System are the fundamental activities that help an organisation establish, maintain and continuously improve the quality of its products and services.

The four major pillars are:

### 1. Quality Planning

Quality Planning involves identifying quality objectives, customer requirements, standards and procedures required to achieve the desired quality.

Example: Defining performance, security and reliability requirements before developing banking software.

### 2. Quality Assurance (QA)

Quality Assurance focuses on preventing defects by ensuring that proper processes, standards and procedures are followed during software development.

Example: Conducting process audits and defining coding standards.

### 3. Quality Control (QC)

Quality Control focuses on identifying defects in the actual software product through testing, inspection and verification.

Example: Testing an application to identify login and payment errors.

### 4. Quality Improvement (QI)

Quality Improvement involves continuously improving software development processes and product quality by analysing defects and customer feedback.

Example: Improving the testing process after identifying repeated software failures.

## Q3. Explain Important Aspects of Quality Management. (May 2023 – 6 Marks, Nov 2024 – 5 Marks)

Definition:

Quality Management is the process of planning, controlling, assuring and improving the quality of software products and development processes to satisfy customer requirements.

Important Aspects of Quality Management:

### 1. Quality Planning

- Defines quality objectives and standards.
- Identifies customer requirements.
- Establishes procedures to achieve the desired quality.

Example: Preparing a software testing plan before application development.

### 2. Quality Assurance

- Focuses on defect prevention.
- Ensures that development processes follow defined standards.
- Includes process audits, reviews and guidelines.

Example: Checking whether developers follow coding standards.

### 3. Quality Control

- Focuses on defect identification.
- Examines the actual software product.
- Uses testing and inspection techniques.

Example: Performing functional testing to identify application defects.

### 4. Quality Improvement

- Focuses on continuous improvement.
- Analyses defects and customer feedback.
- Improves existing development and testing processes.

Example: Improving software performance after analysing customer complaints.

### 5. Customer Satisfaction

- Ensures that software meets customer expectations.
- Collects customer feedback.
- Improves product usability and reliability.

Example: Updating an application based on user feedback.

### 6. Quality Standards

- Ensures compliance with recognised quality standards.
- Provides guidelines for software development and management.
- Helps maintain consistency in organisational processes.

Example: Following ISO 9001 quality management requirements.

Conclusion: Quality Management combines planning, assurance, control, improvement and customer satisfaction to deliver reliable and high-quality software.

## Q4. Important Quality Management Principles

Additional preparation

ISO 9001 is based on seven Quality Management Principles. These are useful if the examiner asks about the principles of quality management rather than its four pillars.

| Principle                         | Explanation                                                  |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1. Customer Focus                 | Understand and satisfy customer requirements.                |
| 2. Leadership                     | Leaders establish quality objectives and direction.          |
| 3. Engagement of People           | Involve employees in achieving quality goals.                |
| 4. Process Approach               | Manage activities as interconnected processes.               |
| 5. Improvement                    | Continuously improve organisational performance.             |
| 6. Evidence-Based Decision Making | Make decisions using data and analysis.                      |
| 7. Relationship Management        | Maintain good relationships with suppliers and stakeholders. |
