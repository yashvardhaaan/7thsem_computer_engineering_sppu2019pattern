## Q1. Explain the Impact of Defects in Different Phases of Software Development. (May 2023 – 6 Marks)

Definition:

A software defect is an error, fault or mistake in a software product that causes it to produce incorrect or unexpected results.

Defects can occur at any stage of the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC). If defects are not identified early, their cost and impact increase in later stages.

### Impact of Defects in Different SDLC Phases

1\. Requirement Analysis Phase

- Incorrect or incomplete requirements may lead to wrong software functionality.
- Misunderstanding customer requirements causes rework.
- Missing requirements can result in customer dissatisfaction.

Example: Not including a password reset requirement in an online banking application.

2\. Design Phase

- Design defects may cause architectural problems.
- Incorrect database design can affect performance.
- Design mistakes can be expensive to correct during implementation.

Example: Designing an application database without proper relationships between customer and transaction tables.

3\. Coding / Implementation Phase

- Programming errors may cause incorrect functionality.
- Logical mistakes can produce unexpected results.
- Security vulnerabilities may be introduced.

Example: Incorrect calculation of the total payment amount in an e-commerce application.

4\. Testing Phase

- Undetected defects may result in software failures.
- More time and effort may be required for debugging.
- Testing delays can affect the project schedule.

Example: A payment failure that was not identified during integration testing.

5\. Deployment Phase

- Defects may cause application crashes in the production environment.
- Incorrect deployment configuration can affect system availability.
- Customers may experience service interruptions.

Example: An application crashes after deployment due to incorrect database configuration.

6\. Maintenance Phase

- Defects may increase maintenance costs.
- Fixing old defects can introduce new defects.
- Repeated failures may reduce customer confidence.

Example: Fixing a payment issue causes an error in the existing order management module.

### Impact of Defects on Software Projects

1. Increased development costs.
2. Delayed project delivery.
3. Reduced software reliability.
4. Increased maintenance effort.
5. Customer dissatisfaction.
6. Loss of business reputation.

Conclusion: Defects in any SDLC phase can negatively affect software quality, cost and delivery time. Therefore, defects should be identified and corrected as early as possible.

## Q2. Explain Customer Satisfaction and Why It Should Be Measured. (May 2023 – 5 Marks)

### 1. Definition of Customer Satisfaction

Customer Satisfaction refers to the level of satisfaction experienced by customers when a software product or service meets or exceeds their expectations and requirements.

It indicates how well the software fulfils customer needs in terms of functionality, quality, reliability and usability.

### 2. Why Should Customer Satisfaction Be Measured?

1\. Understand Customer Requirements

Helps organisations understand whether the software meets customer expectations.

2\. Improve Software Quality

Customer feedback helps identify problems and areas requiring improvement.

3\. Increase Customer Loyalty

Satisfied customers are more likely to continue using the software.

4\. Identify Defects and Problems

Feedback helps identify usability issues, missing features and performance problems.

5\. Improve Business Reputation

High customer satisfaction improves the organisation's reputation and credibility.

6\. Support Continuous Improvement

Measurement helps organisations make better decisions regarding future software improvements.

### 3. Methods of Measuring Customer Satisfaction

1. Customer Surveys: Collect feedback through questionnaires.
2. Feedback Forms: Allow users to report problems and suggestions.
3. Interviews: Collect detailed opinions from customers.
4. Ratings and Reviews: Analyse customer ratings and comments.
5. Complaint Analysis: Study customer complaints to identify recurring problems.

### 4. Example

Consider an online food delivery application.

The company measures customer satisfaction through:

- Ratings after food delivery.
- Feedback about delivery time.
- Complaints about payment failures.
- Reviews about application usability.

Based on this feedback, the company improves delivery tracking, payment functionality and user experience.

Conclusion: Measuring customer satisfaction helps organisations understand customer expectations, improve software quality, increase customer loyalty and achieve business success.

## Q3. Explain Different Characteristics of Software. (Nov 2023 – 6 Marks)

Definition:

Software is a collection of programs, procedures, data and related documentation that instructs a computer to perform specific tasks.

Software has certain characteristics that distinguish it from physical hardware.

### Characteristics of Software

1\. Software is Intangible

Software is a logical product that cannot be physically touched like hardware.

Example: A banking application or operating system.

2\. Software is Developed, Not Manufactured

Software is created through requirement analysis, design, coding and testing. Its reproduction cost is very low compared to its development cost.

3\. Software Does Not Wear Out

Unlike hardware, software does not physically wear out with usage. However, it may deteriorate due to modifications, changing requirements and accumulated defects.

4\. Software is Highly Complex

Software contains many interconnected modules, functions and logical operations, making it difficult to develop and maintain.

5\. Software is Easy to Modify

Software can be modified to add new features, fix defects and meet changing requirements. However, modifications may introduce new defects.

6\. Software Requires Maintenance

Software requires continuous maintenance to correct defects, improve performance and adapt to changing environments.

7\. Software is Mostly Custom-Built

Many software systems are developed according to specific customer requirements, although reusable components and standard software products are also common.

8\. Software Does Not Have Physical Manufacturing Costs

Once software is developed, additional copies can generally be produced at very low cost.

Example:

Consider an online shopping application.

- It is intangible because it exists as code.
- It is developed through programming.
- It can be updated with new payment methods.
- It requires maintenance to fix bugs.
- It contains multiple interconnected modules.

Conclusion: Software is an intangible, complex and modifiable product that requires systematic development, testing and maintenance to ensure quality and reliability.
