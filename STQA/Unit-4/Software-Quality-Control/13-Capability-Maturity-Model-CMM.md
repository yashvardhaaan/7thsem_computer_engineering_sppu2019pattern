## 1. Definition of CMM

The Capability Maturity Model (CMM) is a process improvement framework developed by the Software Engineering Institute (SEI) to evaluate and improve the maturity of an organisation's software development processes.

It consists of five maturity levels, starting from an unstructured process and progressing towards continuous process improvement.

### Level 1: Initial

- Software development processes are unorganised and unpredictable.
- No standard procedures are followed.
- Success depends mainly on individual efforts and skills.
- Projects may face cost and schedule problems.

Example: A software company develops applications without following any defined development process.

### Level 2: Repeatable

- Basic project management processes are established.
- Previous successful practices can be repeated.
- Project planning and tracking are performed.
- Requirements and software configurations are managed.

Example: A company follows project plans and tracks progress to complete similar projects successfully.

### Level 3: Defined

- Standard software development processes are documented and established throughout the organisation.
- Employees follow defined procedures.
- Training is provided to employees.
- Processes are tailored according to project requirements.

Example: An organisation follows a documented Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) for all projects.

### Level 4: Managed

- Software processes are measured and quantitatively controlled.
- Performance is monitored using metrics.
- Product quality and process performance are evaluated.
- Decisions are based on measurable data.

Example: A company measures defect density, project cost, development time and productivity to control software quality.

### Level 5: Optimizing

- Focuses on continuous process improvement.
- Identifies and prevents defects.
- Uses feedback and analysis to improve processes.
- Adopts new technologies and improved development methods.

Example: A company analyses previous software defects and improves its development process to prevent similar defects in future projects.

## 3. Summary of Five CMM Levels

| Level | Name       | Main Focus               |
| ----- | ---------- | ------------------------ |
| 1     | Initial    | Unpredictable processes  |
| 2     | Repeatable | Basic project management |
| 3     | Defined    | Standardised processes   |
| 4     | Managed    | Quantitative measurement |
| 5     | Optimizing | Continuous improvement   |

## 4. Advantages of CMM

1. Improves software development processes.
2. Increases software quality and reliability.
3. Reduces project risks and defects.
4. Improves productivity and cost management.
5. Helps organisations achieve continuous improvement.
6. Increases customer satisfaction.

Conclusion: CMM provides a structured approach to improving software development processes through five maturity levels, from Initial to Optimizing, resulting in better quality, productivity and reliability.
