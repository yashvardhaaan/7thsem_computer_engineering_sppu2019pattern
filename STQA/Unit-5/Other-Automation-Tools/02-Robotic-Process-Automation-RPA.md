## 1. What is Robotic Process Automation (RPA)?

Robotic Process Automation (RPA) is a technology that uses software robots (bots) to automate repetitive, rule-based and time-consuming business tasks that are normally performed by humans.

RPA bots interact with applications, websites, databases and documents through user interfaces or available APIs to perform tasks automatically.

RPA does not necessarily require physical robots or advanced artificial intelligence.

Example: A software bot can automatically read invoices, extract customer details, enter information into an accounting system and generate reports.

## 2. Features of RPA

1\. Automation of Repetitive Tasks

- Automates routine and repetitive business activities.
- Reduces manual effort.

2\. Rule-Based Processing

- Performs tasks according to predefined rules and instructions.
- Suitable for structured and predictable processes.

3\. Accuracy

- Reduces human errors during repetitive data entry and processing.
- Improves consistency.

4\. Faster Execution

- Performs tasks faster than manual processing.
- Can execute multiple tasks simultaneously, depending on the platform and setup.

5\. Non-Invasive Technology

- Can interact with existing applications without requiring major changes to their internal code.
- Often works through user interfaces.

6\. Scalability

- Additional bots can be deployed when workload increases.
- Supports automation of large volumes of transactions.

7\. Integration

\- Can interact with websites, databases, spreadsheets, emails and enterprise applications.

The RPA process involves the following steps:

Step 1: Process Identification

- Identify repetitive, rule-based and high-volume tasks.
- Select suitable business processes for automation.

Step 2: Process Analysis

- Study the existing manual process.
- Identify inputs, outputs, rules and exceptions.

Step 3: Bot Development

- Create software bots using an RPA platform.
- Define the sequence of actions and business rules.

Step 4: Testing

- Test the bot to verify that it performs the required activities correctly.
- Identify and correct errors.

Step 5: Deployment

- Deploy the bot into the required business environment.
- Configure access permissions and credentials securely.

Step 6: Execution and Monitoring

- The bot performs assigned tasks automatically.
- Monitor execution, maintain logs and handle exceptions.

The main components of RPA architecture are:

1\. RPA Developer / Studio

- Used to design, develop and configure automation workflows.
- Example: UiPath Studio.

2\. Software Robots (Bots)

- Execute the automated tasks.
- Interact with applications and process data.

3\. Orchestrator / Control Room

- Manages and schedules bots.
- Monitors execution and provides reports.

4\. Applications and Data Sources

- These are the systems with which bots interact.
- Examples: Websites, Excel, databases, ERP and CRM systems.

## 5. Types of RPA

1\. Attended RPA

- Bots work with human assistance.
- They are triggered by users when required.
- Example: A customer support employee uses a bot to retrieve customer information.

2\. Unattended RPA

- Bots execute tasks independently without continuous human involvement.
- They can run according to a schedule or predefined trigger.
- Example: A bot processes thousands of invoices overnight.

3\. Hybrid RPA

- Combines attended and unattended automation.
- Humans and bots work together to complete a business process.
- Example: A human verifies an invoice, and a bot processes the approved payment information.

## 7. Advantages of RPA

1. Reduces manual work and operational costs.
2. Improves accuracy and consistency.
3. Increases productivity.
4. Provides faster task execution.
5. Works continuously, including outside normal working hours.
6. Improves process efficiency.
7. Can be scaled according to business requirements.

## 8. Limitations of RPA

1. Not suitable for tasks requiring complex human judgment.
2. Bots may fail when application interfaces change.
3. Initial development and maintenance require investment.
4. Poorly designed processes can lead to automation failures.
5. Requires proper security and access control.

## 10. Example of RPA in Banking

Suppose a bank receives 1,000 customer loan applications every day.

A software bot can:

1. Read customer application details.
2. Extract information from documents.
3. Verify whether mandatory fields are completed.
4. Enter customer details into the banking system.
5. Generate a processing report.
6. Notify the concerned employee about incomplete applications.

This reduces manual effort and speeds up loan application processing. Final loan approval can still be performed by a human.

## 11. Conclusion

Robotic Process Automation is a technology that uses software bots to automate repetitive, rule-based business activities. It improves productivity, accuracy and operational efficiency while reducing manual effort. RPA is widely used in banking, healthcare, finance, education and other industries.
