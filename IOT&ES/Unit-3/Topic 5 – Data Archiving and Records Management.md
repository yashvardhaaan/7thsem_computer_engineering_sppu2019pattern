## **1. Data Archiving**

### **Meaning**

Data archiving is the process of transferring old, inactive or less frequently used data from primary storage to a separate, secure storage system for long-term preservation.

In IoT systems, large amounts of data are generated by sensors, smart devices, gateways and applications. All this data cannot be stored in primary storage permanently. Therefore, important historical data is archived for future reference, analysis and compliance.

### **Objectives of Data Archiving**

1. Preserve important historical IoT data.
2. Reduce the storage load on primary systems.
3. Maintain data for future analysis.
4. Support legal and regulatory requirements.
5. Protect archived information from unauthorized access.
6. Ensure long-term availability of important records.

### **Steps involved in Data Archiving**

**1. Data Identification**

- Identify data that is no longer frequently accessed.
- Select important historical records.
- Determine which information needs long-term preservation.

**2. Data Classification**

- Classify data according to importance and sensitivity.
- Identify confidential, operational and general information.
- Determine suitable storage requirements.

**3. Data Selection and Preparation**

- Select records that need to be archived.
- Remove unnecessary duplicate information.
- Organize data before transferring it.

**4. Secure Data Transfer**

- Transfer selected information to archival storage.
- Use secure communication and encryption.
- Prevent unauthorized access during transfer.

**5. Secure Storage**

- Store archived data in secure storage systems.
- Apply access controls and encryption.
- Maintain backup copies where appropriate.

**6. Data Integrity Verification**

- Verify that archived data has not been modified or corrupted.
- Use checksums or cryptographic hashes.
- Maintain data integrity throughout storage.

**7. Retrieval and Retention**

- Retrieve archived information when required.
- Maintain records according to retention policies.
- Securely dispose of information after its retention period expires, where permitted.

### **Advantages of Data Archiving**

1. Reduces primary storage requirements.
2. Preserves historical IoT information.
3. Supports future data analysis.
4. Helps meet compliance requirements.
5. Improves long-term data management.
6. Protects important organizational information.

### **Example**

A smart city generates traffic sensor data every day. Recent data is stored in primary databases for immediate analysis, while older traffic records are transferred to archival storage for historical analysis and future planning.

## **2. Records Management**

### **Meaning**

Records management is the systematic process of creating, organizing, storing, maintaining, retrieving and disposing of organizational records throughout their lifecycle.

In IoT security, records may include device logs, security reports, access records, configuration changes, incident reports and maintenance information.

### **Objectives of Records Management**

1. Maintain accurate and organized records.
2. Ensure easy retrieval of information.
3. Protect records from unauthorized access.
4. Maintain data integrity and confidentiality.
5. Support audits and investigations.
6. Follow organizational retention policies.

### **Activities involved in Records Management**

**1. Record Creation**

- Generate records from IoT devices and applications.
- Maintain security logs and operational information.
- Record important system activities.

**2. Record Classification**

- Categorize records according to their type and importance.
- Identify confidential and sensitive records.
- Apply suitable security controls.

**3. Record Storage**

- Store records in secure databases or storage systems.
- Apply encryption and access control.
- Maintain backups to prevent information loss.

**4. Record Maintenance**

- Keep records accurate and updated.
- Protect records against unauthorized modification.
- Maintain integrity and availability.

**5. Record Retrieval**

- Provide authorized users with access to required records.
- Use proper indexing and searching mechanisms.
- Retrieve information during audits and investigations.

**6. Record Retention**

- Define how long different records must be maintained.
- Follow organizational and regulatory requirements.
- Prevent premature deletion of important records.

**7. Record Disposal**

- Identify records that have completed their retention period.
- Obtain appropriate authorization for disposal.
- Securely delete or destroy records.
- Maintain disposal documentation where required.

### **Advantages**

- Improves information organization.
- Supports security audits.
- Helps in incident investigation.
- Ensures accountability.
- Protects confidential information.
- Supports legal and regulatory compliance.

## **3. Difference between Data Archiving and Records Management**

| Data Archiving                                     | Records Management                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Focuses on preserving inactive or historical data. | Manages records throughout their entire lifecycle.       |
| Mainly deals with long-term storage.               | Deals with creation, storage, retrieval and disposal.    |
| Reduces primary storage usage.                     | Maintains organized and controlled records.              |
| Supports historical analysis.                      | Supports audits, investigations and accountability.      |
| Example: Archiving old sensor readings.            | Example: Maintaining security logs and incident reports. |

## **4. Security Considerations**

While archiving data and managing records, the following security measures should be implemented:

1. **Encryption:** Protect archived information from unauthorized access.
2. **Access Control:** Allow only authorized users to access records.
3. **Data Integrity:** Use hashes and checksums to detect modifications.
4. **Backup:** Maintain backup copies to prevent data loss.
5. **Retention Policy:** Define how long information should be preserved.
6. **Secure Disposal:** Permanently remove records when they are no longer required and disposal is authorized.
7. **Audit Trails:** Maintain logs of record access, modification and deletion.

## **5. Conclusion**

Data archiving and records management are important components of IoT security. Data archiving preserves historical information for long-term use, whereas records management controls organizational records throughout their lifecycle. Both ensure data availability, integrity, confidentiality and compliance.
