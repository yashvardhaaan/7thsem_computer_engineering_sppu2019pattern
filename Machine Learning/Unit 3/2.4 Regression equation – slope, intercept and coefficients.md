# **Regression Equation: Slope, Intercept and Coefficients**

In the previous topic, we studied Simple and Multiple Linear Regression. Now we will understand the parameters that make up their equations.

For Simple Linear Regression, the equation is:

\\[ \boxed{\hat Y=b_0+b_1X} \\]

Here, \\(b_0\\) and \\(b_1\\) determine the position and direction of the regression line.

We will study three concepts:

1. Intercept (\\(b_0\\))
2. Slope (\\(b_1\\))
3. Regression coefficient and its interpretation

## **1. Intercept (**\\(b_0\\)**)**

**Definition:**

The intercept is the predicted value of the dependent variable \\(Y\\) when the independent variable \\(X\\) is equal to zero.

In the equation:

\\[ \hat Y=b_0+b_1X \\]

\\(b_0\\) represents the intercept.

**Example:**

Suppose the regression equation is:

\\[ \hat Y=20+5X \\]

When \\(X=0\\):

\\[ \hat Y=20+5(0)=20 \\]

Therefore, the intercept is **20**.

It means that the model predicts a value of 20 for Y when X is zero.

## **2. Slope (**\\(b_1\\)**)**

**Definition:**

The slope is the change in the predicted dependent variable for a one-unit increase in the independent variable.

In the equation:

\\[ \hat Y=b_0+b_1X \\]

\\(b_1\\) represents the slope.

For the example:

\\[ \hat Y=20+5X \\]

The slope is 5.

This means that for every one-unit increase in X, the predicted value of Y increases by 5 units.

### **Types of slope**

- Positive slope: Y increases as X increases.
- Negative slope: Y decreases as X increases.
- Zero slope: Predicted Y remains constant as X changes.

## **2.2 Formula for calculating slope and intercept**

For a dataset containing \\(n\\) observations, the slope of the Simple Linear Regression line can be calculated using:

\\[ \boxed{ b_1= \frac{n\sum XY-\sum X\sum Y} {n\sum X^2-(\sum X)^2} } \\]

The intercept is:

\\[ \boxed{b_0=\bar Y-b_1\bar X} \\]

Where:

- \\(n\\) = Number of observations.
- \\(\sum X\\) = Sum of all X values.
- \\(\sum Y\\) = Sum of all Y values.
- \\(\sum XY\\) = Sum of products of corresponding X and Y values.
- \\(\sum X^2\\) = Sum of squared X values.
- \\(\bar X\\) = Mean of X values.
- \\(\bar Y\\) = Mean of Y values.

These formulas calculate the coefficients of the least-squares regression line. We will study the full Least Square Method and its procedure separately.

## **2.3 Solved illustrative example**

Suppose the following data is given:

| X | Y |
| - | - |
| 1 | 3 |
| 2 | 5 |
| 3 | 7 |
| 4 | 9 |

Find the regression equation.

**Step 1: Prepare the calculation table.**

| X      | Y      | X²     | XY     |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | ------ |
| 1      | 3      | 1      | 3      |
| 2      | 5      | 4      | 10     |
| 3      | 7      | 9      | 21     |
| 4      | 9      | 16     | 36     |
| **10** | **24** | **30** | **70** |

Therefore:

\\[ n=4,\quad \sum X=10 \\]

\\[ \sum Y=24,\quad \sum X^2=30 \\]

\\[ \sum XY=70 \\]

**Step 2: Calculate slope (**\\(b_1\\)**).**

\\[ b_1= \frac{4(70)-(10)(24)} {4(30)-(10)^2} \\]

\\[ =\frac{280-240}{120-100} \\]

\\[ =\frac{40}{20} \\]

\\[ \boxed{b_1=2} \\]

**Step 3: Calculate intercept (**\\(b_0\\)**).**

First calculate the means:

\\[ \bar X=\frac{10}{4}=2.5 \\]

\\[ \bar Y=\frac{24}{4}=6 \\]

Now:

\\[ b_0=\bar Y-b_1\bar X \\]

\\[ =6-(2)(2.5) \\]

\\[ \boxed{b_0=1} \\]

**Step 4: Write the regression equation.**

\\[ \hat Y=b_0+b_1X \\]

Final answer

\\[ \boxed{\hat Y=1+2X} \\]

**Interpretation:**

- Intercept = 1.
- Slope = 2.
- For every one-unit increase in X, predicted Y increases by 2 units.

For example, when \\(X=5\\):

\\[ \hat Y=1+2(5)=11 \\]

Predicted Y = 11.

## **2.4 Regression coefficients in Multiple Linear Regression**

In Multiple Linear Regression, the equation is:

\\[ \hat Y=b_0+b_1X_1+b_2X_2+\cdots+b_nX_n \\]

Here:

- \\(b_0\\) = Intercept.
- \\(b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_n\\) = Regression coefficients.
- \\(X_1,X_2,\ldots,X_n\\) = Independent variables.

**Example:**

\\[ \hat Y=10+4X_1+3X_2 \\]

Suppose:

- \\(X_1\\) = Study hours.
- \\(X_2\\) = Practice tests completed.
- \\(Y\\) = Predicted marks.

Interpretation:

| Parameter    | Interpretation                                                                                |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| \\(b_0=10\\) | Predicted marks when both inputs are zero.                                                    |
| \\(b_1=4\\)  | Predicted marks increase by 4 for one additional study hour, keeping practice tests constant. |
| \\(b_2=3\\)  | Predicted marks increase by 3 for one additional practice test, keeping study hours constant. |

**Important:** In Multiple Linear Regression, each coefficient describes the relationship with its corresponding independent variable while keeping the other independent variables constant.

## **2.5 Difference between slope and intercept**

| Basis             | Slope                                                      | Intercept                                      |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Meaning           | Rate of change in predicted Y.                             | Predicted Y when X is zero.                    |
| Symbol            | \\(b_1\\)                                                  | \\(b_0\\)                                      |
| Position          | Determines line's direction and steepness.                 | Determines where line crosses Y-axis.          |
| Positive/negative | Can be positive, negative or zero.                         | Can be positive, negative or zero.             |
| Example           | \\(b_1=5\\) means Y increases by 5 per unit increase in X. | \\(b_0=20\\) means predicted Y is 20 at X = 0. |

## **2.6 Important examination points**

1. The slope is also called the regression coefficient in Simple Linear Regression.
2. The intercept is the constant term of the regression equation.
3. The regression equation is used to estimate the dependent variable.
4. A positive slope indicates an increasing predicted relationship.
5. A negative slope indicates a decreasing predicted relationship.
6. A zero slope indicates a constant predicted value.
7. Regression coefficients are learned from training data.
8. The least-squares method is commonly used to estimate the coefficients.

## **2.8 Exam-ready answer (6 marks)**

**Q. Explain the regression equation and the significance of slope and intercept.**

Linear Regression establishes a mathematical relationship between independent and dependent variables.

The Simple Linear Regression equation is:

\\[ \hat Y=b_0+b_1X \\]

Where:

- \\(\hat Y\\) is the predicted dependent variable.
- \\(X\\) is the independent variable.
- \\(b_0\\) is the intercept.
- \\(b_1\\) is the slope or regression coefficient.

**Slope:** It indicates the change in predicted Y for a one-unit increase in X. A positive slope indicates an increasing relationship, while a negative slope indicates a decreasing relationship.

**Intercept:** It represents the predicted value of Y when X is zero.

The coefficients can be calculated using:

\\[ b_1= \frac{n\sum XY-\sum X\sum Y} {n\sum X^2-(\sum X)^2} \\]

\\[ b_0=\bar Y-b_1\bar X \\]

**Example:** For the equation \\(Y=20+5X\\), the intercept is 20 and the slope is 5. Thus, the predicted value of Y increases by 5 units for every one-unit increase in X.

**Conclusion:** Slope and intercept are important parameters used to construct the regression line and predict continuous numerical values.
